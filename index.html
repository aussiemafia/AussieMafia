THE FAIR PLAY CODE

Good rivals. Fair rules.

01PvP & raiding+

PvP and raiding are part of Rust. Fight hard and play fair. Do not repeatedly target or attack the same player or team.

02No cheating. No shortcuts.+

No cheats, scripts, hacks, exploits or unfair advantages.

03Keep the island playable+

No base griefing, no Tool Cupboard griefing and no monument blocking.

04Raid sealing: one hour+

Temporary sealing during a raid is allowed for the first hour only. After one hour, all raid-sealing structures must be removed.

05Respect the people+

No racism or extreme toxicity. Keep the rivalry in the game and respect the people behind the players.

06Independent administration+

Admins and moderators do not play on the map. Their role is to manage the server, not compete with players.

PLAN YOUR WAY IN

How much boom?

Add the doors and walls in your raid path. Compare the explosives you’ll need.

Your raid path

Full-health targets · Vanilla PC Rust

Each target is breached separately. Totals do not assume splash damage through several targets. Explosive ammo assumes direct rifle hits; missed shots, repairs and server modifiers need extra boom.

Your boom budget

Propane explosive bombs (gas bottles): placed and catapult totals are separate. Counts assume full-health targets and direct damage; placement and blast obstruction can change the result. Each bomb costs 900 sulfur, 20 low grade fuel and one empty propane tank.

Each comparison is a complete alternative loadout. Sulfur includes gunpowder and explosives; weapons and other crafting materials are extra.

How to use the results & data sources

Choose a route through a base, add each barrier, then adjust quantities. A double door is one target, not two individual panels. For example, two garage doors require 6 regular rockets on their own, or 300 explosive rounds on their own. The mixed-door option finishes rocket damage with explosive ammo.

Rockets can damage adjacent building pieces, while C4 concentrates damage on its attached target. Satchels can fail to ignite. Keep spare ammunition and allow for defenders repairing.

Raid figures are estimates and can change after updates. Rocket item reference · Propane explosive item reference.

MONUMENT RECON

Eyes on the island.

Click a camera code to copy it. Sit at a Computer Station, paste it into the identifier field, then select Add.

Codes are case-sensitive. Cargo feeds are available while the ship is on the map. Missing monuments or cameras show no signal.
Map-specific codes & sources

Underwater Labs and Abandoned Military Base use map-specific identifiers. Find the complete identifier at the monument’s Computer Station; there is no universal number to paste for every server. Not every monument has public CCTV.

ARMOURY / KNOW YOUR WEAPONS

Rust Weapons.

Choose a weapon to compare ammunition, see compatible attachments and plan a setup.

How to read damage, range and recoil

Figures are the Facepunch-hosted Rust wiki’s listed ammo compatibility values, checked 5 October 2026. They are not guaranteed damage against an armoured player. Hit location, distance, projectile count, attachments, fire and explosive effects can change the result. Shotgun damage is listed per pellet; slugs are single projectiles. These figures are not building raid costs—use the Raid Calculator for those.

Bullet drop is downward projectile travel; damage falloff is damage lost over distance. Higher projectile speed generally reduces travel time. A reliable metre-by-metre drop chart also needs the weapon’s gravity, drag, sight zero and current game version; no invented drop distances are shown.

Exact repeatable recoil traces have not been verified for this guide. Rust’s automatic recoil includes variation. Mouse travel also depends on sensitivity, aiming mode, stance and attachments. General recoil advice cannot guarantee that every bullet lands on target.
